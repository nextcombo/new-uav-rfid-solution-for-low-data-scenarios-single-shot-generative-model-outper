# New UAV RFID Solution for Low Data Scenarios: Single-Shot Generative Model Outperforms GAN/VA [with Python Code]

Low data scenario for drone radio frequency identification: Single-shot generative models outperform GAN/VA
Abstract: This study addresses the challenge of using radio frequency fingerprinting to identify drones in restricted radio frequency environments. Due to environmental interference and hardware defects, the complexity and variability of radio frequency signals often render traditional radio frequency identification methods ineffective. To address these issues, this research systematically applies single-shot generative methods to enhance transformed radio frequency signals, significantly improving the performance of drone recognition. This method demonstrates excellent performance in low data scenarios, outperforming deep generative models such as conditional generative adversarial networks (GAN) and variational autoencoders (VAE). The paper theoretically proves the effectiveness of single-shot generative models in enhancing limited data, laying a precedent for their application in constrained radio frequency environments. This study provides a new direction for the development of machine learning technology in low data scenarios, and the technique can also be applied to non-traditional complex sequence analysis beyond images and videos.
Keywords: Low data scenario; Wasserstein distance; synthetic data; out-of-distribution data; extrapolation method
Introduction: Drones (commonly referred to as unmanned aerial vehicles) are increasingly being integrated into various fields such as surveillance and logistics, highlighting the urgency of network security protection for them. Therefore, distinguishing legitimate from malicious drone devices using radio frequency fingerprinting is crucial, especially in various constrained environments. Factors such as temperature, humidity, environmental obstruction, and hardware defects introduce complexity and variability to radio frequency signals, posing significant challenges to the stable recognition and tracking of drones. These factors interfere with the clarity and intensity of radio frequency signals, creating constrained radio frequency environments where available signal quantity decreases. While traditional radio frequency identification methods are effective in open environments, they often fail to function effectively in dense vegetation, urban canyons, harsh weather conditions, or under hardware failure, making reliable detection and classification of drones a major challenge.
The advent of machine learning technology has opened new avenues for enhancing the recognition performance of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) in such complex radio frequency (RF) environments. By leveraging deep learning algorithms, it is possible to extract and analyze complex patterns that are often obscured or distorted by environmental factors in RF signals. However, data-intensive deep learning methods themselves cannot address the issue of data scarcity in damaged environments, necessitating the development of reliable techniques to enhance RF signals or their transformed forms when data is limited. The current challenge primarily lies in the fact that existing data enhancement techniques are primarily designed for computer vision applications. As signal semantic features are complex, extending them to other types of sequence data often fails to achieve the desired results.
Inspired by computer vision technology, this study systematically adopts a single-shot generative approach to enhance discrete Fourier transform (DFT) RF signals under different training data scales, thereby simulating the low data scenario brought about by constrained RF environments. Experimental results show that this method significantly improves various evaluation metrics for UAV recognition, outperforming other frameworks such as conditional generative adversarial networks (GANs) and variational autoencoders (VAEs) in handling low data scenarios.
The structure of this study is as follows: Firstly, we explore the capability of single-shot generative models in enhancing sequence datasets, clarifying the mathematical definitions of sequences, subsequences, single-shot generative models, and distance metrics. We determine the upper bound of Wasserstein distance between the true sequence and the generated sequence based on the expected distance of subsequences, and verify this general theory through intuitive special cases of both sequences and subsequences; secondly, we detail the UAV recognition experiments, including the selected dataset, single-shot generative model, conditional deep generative model as a benchmark, classification pipeline, and evaluation methods; finally, we analyze the experimental results, covering the accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score of classification tasks across all design scenarios and methods.
Methodology Overview The core methodology of this study revolves around single-shot generative distribution matching, combining theoretical derivations with experimental validation, to develop a UAV recognition scheme suitable for RF signal enhancement. The core method modules include the following aspects:
1.
Foundational Construct: The paper defines core concepts such as sequences, subsequences, and selection matrices. It adopts the Wasserstein distance (geodesic distance) as a measure of distribution similarity, proposes the mathematical definition of single-shot generative models, and derives an upper bound for the Wasserstein distance between real data and synthetic data through lemmas and theorems. Additionally, it presents specific inferences for two-dimensional sequences (such as matrixized radio frequency signals), providing theoretical assurance for the fidelity of distribution in synthetic data.
2  
Core Generative Model: Generative Patch Matching (GPDM): Utilizes GPDM as the single-pass generative model. This model achieves distribution matching by minimizing the slice Wasserstein distance (SWD) between real and generated samples. It employs a multi-scale hierarchical strategy, allowing for the learning of sample global structures early in training. It is an unmodeled method, offering higher efficiency in generating multiple samples compared to other single-pass generative models.
3  
Comparative benchmark models: Select conditional generative adversarial networks (CGAN) and conditional variational autoencoders (CVAE) as deep generative baseline models, and optimize and improve upon them - CGAN integrates the Wasserstein GAN with gradient penalty to enhance training stability, while CVAE employs a cyclic annealing strategy to balance KL divergence loss; simultaneously, data cleaning is performed on the training data (using the MAD method to remove outliers), and mixup data augmentation is applied to address overfitting and mode collapse issues in low-data scenarios.
4  
Data Processing and Classification Flow: After DFT transformation of the RF signals, 2025 frequency features are extracted to reconstruct a 45×45 matrix for single-shot generation model adaptation; 5-fold cross-validation is used to divide the dataset, simulating scenarios with 10% to 50% low data; The classification end combines SMOTE oversampling to address class imbalance issues, using XGBoost classifiers to identify drone types and flight modes, with accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score as core evaluation metrics.
5  
Experimental Design: Two major tasks are set up, including 4 categories (UAV presence + type) and 10 categories (UAV presence + type + flight mode). Each task includes five low data scenarios with 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%, and 50% training data. A total of 5000 synthetic samples are generated for all models to ensure fair comparisons.
Conclusion: This study confirms that single-shot generative patch distribution matching (GPDM) and other single-shot generative models can significantly improve UAV classification metrics in low data scenarios where traditional deep generative models struggle to perform effectively. The study systematically evaluates the application of single-shot generative methods in radio frequency signal transformation synthesis, constructing and validating an upper bound on the distribution distance between real and synthetic data. The experimental results are consistent with theoretical predictions across various scenarios, demonstrating the generative robustness of the method in distribution matching, ensuring the fidelity of synthetic data for reliable UAV recognition. Moreover, in tasks involving limited data and highly complex signals, the performance of single-shot generative data augmentation methods significantly outperforms conditional generative adversarial networks (CGAN) and conditional variational autoencoders (CVAE). Overall, this study provides a rigorous and efficient solution for enhancing UAV recognition performance in constrained radio frequency environments.
